Questions people ask first

Which refrigerants does it cover?
Five: R-410A, R-32, R-454B, R-22 and R-134a.
Why does superheat use the dew point and subcooling the bubble point?
Superheat is measured on the suction line, where the refrigerant has just finished boiling, so it is read against the dew point: suction line temperature minus dew point. Subcooling is measured on the liquid line, where it has just finished condensing, so it is read against the bubble point: bubble point minus liquid line temperature. Glidechart picks the curve from the side you type on, so there is no switch to set.
What is glide?
On a blend such as R-410A or R-454B, the dew point and the bubble point at one pressure differ. That difference is the glide. With Glidechart Pro, Glidechart shows it under the saturation temperature on a blend, for example “Glide at 107.5 psig: 2.7 °F” on R-454B. On a single compound (R-32, R-22, R-134a) the two points are the same, so there is no glide line.
Where do the numbers come from?
Every value comes from CoolProp, the open-source thermophysical property library (Bell, Wronski, Quoilin and Lemort, 2014): the app carries a table CoolProp computed every 0.1 °F and reads it on your phone. Each result names it in the working under it. R-454B is modelled as 68.9% R-32 and 31.1% R-1234yf by mass. About in the app shows the version Glidechart uses.
Can I look up a pressure from a temperature?
Yes. Type a temperature in the Dew point or Bubble point field and the pressure appears. On the PT chart, switch between By °F and By psig (By psig is part of Glidechart Pro).
Why does it say “No saturation point”?
The pressure you typed has no saturation point for that refrigerant, which is usually a mistyped digit. Check the digits.
What does a negative psig mean?
Gauge pressure is measured from one standard atmosphere, 14.696 psia. A negative psig is below atmospheric, a vacuum on the gauge.
Can I use °C, kPa or bar, or correct for altitude?
Not in this version. Glidechart works in °F and psig, with gauge pressure from one standard atmosphere.
Does it tell me whether the charge is right?
No. Glidechart gives readings. It does not judge a charge. Compare the superheat and subcooling with the equipment manufacturer’s charging chart. Reference tool only — verify against manufacturer specs and site conditions before charging.
Does it need a signal or an account?
Neither. It works in airplane mode, and there is no account or sign-in.
I cleared my readings by mistake.
Tap Undo, which stays on screen until your next edit.
How do I copy a reading?
With Glidechart Pro, long-press a result and choose Copy. The copy names the refrigerant, the curve and the source.
Does it work with large text and screen readers?
Yes: Dynamic Type and VoiceOver on iPhone and iPad, font size and TalkBack on Android, and dark mode on both.
Does it cost anything?
The app is a free download with Readings for all five refrigerants and the PT chart by °F. Glidechart Pro is one in-app purchase, with no subscription and no ads, that adds the chart by psig, the glide at your pressure and copy with source. The store shows its price.
I bought Glidechart Pro on another phone, or reinstalled.
Open About, then Glidechart Pro, and tap Restore Purchase, signed in to the same Apple Account or Google account you bought it with.
